**The Lord’s Table: From Adam’s Emptiness to Christ’s Fullness**

**Suggested readings:** Romans 5:12, 17–19; 1 Corinthians 15:45–49; Colossians 2:9–10; 1 Corinthians 11:23–26.

Brothers and sisters, as we come to the Lord’s Table, Scripture sets before us two men: **Adam** and **Jesus Christ**.

Adam was the first man, the head of the old humanity. Through Adam’s disobedience, sin entered the world, and with sin came death, condemnation, alienation, shame, and emptiness. What Adam received from God was good—life, fellowship, dominion, and the image of God—but through unbelief and disobedience he lost the enjoyment of it all. In Adam, we find the tragedy of a human life turned away from its source. [study](https://study.bible/lesson/561)

But God did not leave humanity in Adam. He sent His Son, Jesus Christ—the last Adam, the true and greater Adam. Where Adam disobeyed, Christ obeyed. Where Adam brought condemnation, Christ brings justification. Where Adam’s act brought death, Christ’s righteous act brings life. Christ did not merely repair a few broken areas of human life; He came to establish a new humanity in Himself. [modernreformation](https://www.modernreformation.org/resources/essays/jesus-the-second-and-greater-adam)

At this table we remember that Jesus became truly human. He entered our weakness, bore our sin, faced death, and gave His body for us. Yet He was not merely another man. In Him “all the fullness of Deity dwells bodily.” Jesus is fully God and fully man—the One in whom God’s fullness and perfect humanity meet. [biblehub](https://biblehub.com/colossians/2-9.htm)

And Colossians says something astonishing: because we are in Him, we have been made complete in Him. The fullness is not in Adam. It is not in ourselves, our achievements, our religious effort, or our ability to improve ourselves. The fullness is in Jesus. [study](https://study.bible/lesson/561)

Adam’s life says, “Take for yourself. Be independent. Find life apart from God.”  
Jesus’ life says, “Receive from God. Trust the Father. Give yourself in love.”

Adam reached for the tree in disobedience and brought death. Jesus went to the tree—the cross—in obedience and brought life.

So when we take the bread, we remember: Christ gave His body so that we might no longer live under Adam’s ruin. His body was given to make us a new people.

When we take the cup, we remember: Christ’s blood speaks of forgiveness, reconciliation, and a new covenant. We do not come because we are full in ourselves; we come because Christ is full of grace, and from His fullness we receive.

Let us therefore come with humility:

- Confessing that in Adam we were empty, fallen, and unable to save ourselves.
- Giving thanks that in Christ we are forgiven, accepted, and made alive.
- Resting not in our own works, but in His finished work.
- Looking forward to the day when we will bear fully the image of the heavenly Man.

As we eat the bread and drink the cup, we proclaim not only Christ’s death, but also this great truth: **we are no longer defined by Adam’s failure. In Christ, the last Adam, we receive life, righteousness, and fullness.** [modernreformation](https://www.modernreformation.org/resources/essays/jesus-the-second-and-greater-adam)

### Prayer

Lord Jesus, we thank You that You are the true and last Adam. Through Adam came sin and death, but through Your obedience, Your body given, and Your blood shed, You have brought forgiveness and life. Turn our hearts away from self-reliance and fill us with faith in You. As we partake of this bread and cup, help us to remember that all fullness is found in You. Amen.
